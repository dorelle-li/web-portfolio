Challenge:
Open-ended play lets children choose, imagine, and negotiate freely, but the conditions for it are shrinking. The project strives to find what kind of foundational element could invite autonomous creation again.

Design:
A dual-layer play system pairing physical building with spatial projection. Children build a story world from open-ended blocks on the mat, and a projection system reads the pieces and extends it onto the surrounding walls in real time.

Outcome:
Projection is treated as an extension of physical play rather than a replacement for it. Children remain the authors of the world, while the system amplifies what they build.

1.Background Research

Unstructured play lets children choose, imagine, and negotiate on their own terms, developing creativity, social skills, and decision-making through a process with no fixed answer.

This project aims to:Reconnect physical play — let children play by touching, arranging, moving, and building with real objects.Support open-ended creation — no preset way to play. Children decide the story, the characters, and the world.

2.Idea Development

Goal:Explore how offering more engaging foundational elements can encourage children’s autonomous creation, imagination, and interaction during open-ended play.

Out of the 3 concepts, we went with the first idea as it offers richer foundational elements for children to create and imagine.
